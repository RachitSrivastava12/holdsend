## Done today

- Android CI follow-ups ([#74](https://github.com/zyakuhq/holdsend/pull/74), [decision 0016](../docs/decisions/0016-android-emulator-ci.md)). The emulator is pinned to one build and checked against the SHA-1 in Google's download list, so a new emulator release cannot break the job without a change here. The system image cannot be pinned the same way, so the job warns when its revision changes. Gradle downloads are cached (about 12 minutes down to about 8.5), and when the job fails, logcat and the emulator log are kept as an artifact for 14 days. The first try failed in CI (avdmanager needs the emulator package to create the device); fixed by creating the device first and swapping the emulator after.
- Dev dependency advisories ([#75](https://github.com/zyakuhq/holdsend/pull/75)): `npm audit fix`, lockfile only. The three high advisories are gone; three moderate ones remain and need a major Expo upgrade. None of the published packages has a third-party runtime dependency.

## LinkedIn post
Today's HoldSend work was not a feature. It was making a test trustworthy. Our Android CI job installs the app on an emulator, kills it, and checks that a background task publishes a waiting note exactly once. Until today it pulled whatever emulator Google released last, so a new release could break it with no change on our side. The lesson: a test that fails for outside reasons teaches people to ignore it, and then it stops protecting anything. What shipped: the emulator is pinned to one build checked against Google's checksum, Gradle downloads are cached, and the device log is kept when a run fails. I also cleared the high severity npm advisories in the example and dev tools. https://github.com/zyakuhq/holdsend
